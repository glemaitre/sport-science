The reader is learning cycling physiology from two lectures, and does not already know the vocabulary.

Energy systems (lecture key `energy-systems`): a contraction spends ATP at three places. Myosin uses one ATP per cross-bridge cycle. The sodium–potassium pump moves 3 Na+ out and 2 K+ in per ATP. SERCA moves 2 Ca2+ back into the sarcoplasmic reticulum per ATP. The ATP pool is about 5–8 mmol/kg of wet muscle. It is a concentration the cell defends, not a fuel tank. From the first pedal stroke it has to be remade about as fast as it is used. The three supplies overlap from the first seconds. Do not teach the sequence "phosphocreatine, then glycolysis, then oxygen." Phosphagen is creatine kinase: phosphocreatine + ADP + H+ makes ATP + creatine. Glycolysis to lactate yields about 3 ATP per glycogen glucosyl unit and about 2 per blood glucose. Lactate is a fuel and a redox valve, not the acid and not a dead end. Oxidative phosphorylation yields about 30 to 36 ATP per glucose. Gross cycling efficiency is about 20–25%. Absolute fat oxidation peaks near 60–65% of VO2max (FatMax). That is not race pace. PMA is the best mean power over about 5 minutes. Critical power is the heavy/severe boundary. It is not the same wattage as lactate threshold.

Mitochondria (lecture key `mitochondria`): the inner membrane is the seal, folded into cristae. The outer membrane is not the proton barrier. Endurance training adds mitochondrial volume and, more tightly linked to VO2max, cristae density. It does not invent a more efficient respiratory enzyme. About 2.5 ATP per mitochondrial NADH and 1.5 per FADH2 once export is counted. How much leak uncoupling protein 3 carries is unsettled.

A paper is useful when the finding transfers to cycling or to a similar continuous endurance sport: running, trail running, rowing, swimming, triathlon, cross-country skiing, race walking. The mechanisms that count are lactate, mitochondria, VO2max, glycogen, thresholds, fuel use, and muscle energetics. Name the sport the subjects actually did. Say what would transfer to the bike. Do not copy the abstract. Do not invent a number that is not in the abstract. One paper does not change the notes.
