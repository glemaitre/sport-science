The reader is a data scientist at a professional cycling team. A paper is worth keeping when it helps the team understand, analyse, interpret, or improve any part of cycling performance. The question is what the team could do with the finding.

Keep work on the rider and the bike in these areas, and in neighbouring ones when the finding is about performance:

- Physiology: energy supply, muscle, lactate, mitochondria, VO2max, thresholds, fatigue, recovery, heat, altitude, and the response to training.
- Nutrition: carbohydrate and fat, glycogen, hydration, supplements, and race-day feeding, when the outcome is performance, recovery, or a measured physiological response.
- Biomechanics: pedalling, position, cadence, force, joint load, and how the rider produces power.
- Aerodynamics: drag, position, equipment, and the trade-off between drag and the power the rider can hold.
- Also training load, pacing, monitoring, and testing, when they change how performance is measured or decided.

Physiology, including the nutrition that is really fuel use, is not limited to cycling. A finding from a similar continuous endurance sport counts when the mechanism would transfer to the bike: running, trail running, rowing, swimming, triathlon, cross-country skiing, race walking. Name the sport the subjects actually did and say what would transfer.

Biomechanics and aerodynamics count when the task is cycling, or when a method or mechanism applies directly to the bike. A running-gait paper, a swim-stroke paper, or a team-sport movement paper does not transfer just because the athletes are fit.

Drop surgery technique, injury-rehab protocols that do not teach a performance mechanism, sport management, match analysis, talent identification, and coaching pedagogy. Drop intermittent team sports unless the finding is one of the mechanisms above. A device, a garment, or a supplement counts only when the measured outcome is performance or a mechanism the team would use.

The learning note says what the paper claims and what the team could use. Do not copy the abstract. Do not invent a number that is not in the abstract. One paper does not change the notes.
